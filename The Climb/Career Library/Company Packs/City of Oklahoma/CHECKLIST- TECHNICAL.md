- **What is your approach to Software development? What are some software development methodologies you know or have worked with? What steps do you take while following them?** 

My approach to developing any piece of software is mostly centered around the following standard software development methodologies to take steps like 

- Understanding the Requirements and checking what’s feasible and what’s not according to various factors like time and budget.

- create high-level designs that meet the requirements and make it scalable. 

- Dividing them into smaller tasks/user stories, planning sprints, 

- Writing clean and maintainable code, while following best practices and coding standards. 

- Writing unit tests and integration tests, for every feature or bug fix.

- Regularly review the progress with the team to gather feedback and make any adjustments before deploying to production and have retrospectives to discuss what went well and what could be improved in the next sprint.

- Having good Documentation, so that it can be understandable and easy to be worked on by others

- And ensure maintenance through bug fixes, and necessary timed updates.

- Some methodologies are:

Agile is an iterative and incremental approach to software development that emphasizes rapid delivery of small, functional segments of the product.  Agile is more flexible and is more customer centric.

Waterfall is a more linear and sequential approach to software development where each phase must be completed before moving on to the next.

**Agile:**

**Scenario: Full Stack Developer at Oklahoma State University**

- **Project:** Enhancing the university's internal portal to improve UI responsiveness and optimize database queries.

- **Implementation:** We followed the Agile methodology to deliver incremental improvements and gather continuous feedback from the university staff and students. The project was divided into several sprints, each focusing on specific features like lazy loading for images and adding indexes to frequently queried fields. This iterative approach allowed us to refine the portal based on user feedback and ensured that each feature was thoroughly tested before deployment.
