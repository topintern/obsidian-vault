**Database Modeling, Persistence, validations (7/10, but good at it):**

Designing database schemas (SQL or NoSQL), optimizing queries, and ensuring data integrity.

**Infrastructure & DevOps (7/10 but good at it)**

Setting up servers, managing cloud services (AWS/GCP), Dockerization, and CI/CD pipelines, IAC, scripting and troubleshooting

**Distributed Systems & Performance (5/10 horrible at it, barebones knowledge)**

Handling background jobs, message queues (like RabbitMQ or Kafka), and caching (like Redis) to make things run fast at scale.

**Agentic AI (10/10)**

Agent Orchestration—managing how multiple AI agents talk to each other to solve a complex task

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): Connecting LLMs to your data (the "Database Modeling" equivalent) so the AI has context.

Chain Management: Using frameworks like LangChain or AutoGPT to sequence AI actions and API calls.

#### 

#### **AI Infrastructure & Data (6/10)**

- **LLMOps:** Setting up CI/CD for models, monitoring for hallucinations and managing model versioning.

- **Vector Databases:** Specialized data persistence (like Pinecone or Weaviate) for storing and retrieving AI-readable data (embeddings).

- **GPU Orchestration:** Managing the heavy compute resources required to run or fine-tune models at scale.

- 

#### **Model Engineering**

This is the most specialized area, often requiring deeper math and research knowledge.

- **Fine-tuning:** Taking a "base" model (like Llama 3) and training it on specific data to make it an expert in a certain field.

- **Evaluation (Eval):** Building rigorous testing frameworks to measure if an AI's performance is actually improving or getting worse.
