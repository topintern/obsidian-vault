## Starting point

The vault contains coding exercises, pattern catalogues, technical references and experience material. Their presence does not establish your current mastery. Begin with a quick diagnostic; skip ahead when the work is genuinely easy.

Topic coverage dates are a proposed route, not a requirement to advance with a weak foundation. A live interview can reorder the syllabus.

## Coding

**3–4 deliberate problems daily** is the target. Distinguish a new attempt, a review, an independent solve and an assisted reconstruction. Aim to explain brute force, invariant/state, complexity and edge cases. Use [[DSA Map]] to cover patterns and [[Interview Question Bank]] for assigned records.

A good 150-minute block: two focused new attempts, two shorter cold reviews, then notes/retry planning. Harder problems may change the mix; don't count reading a solution as solving it.

## Experience and behavioral

Normal budget: **1 h**. Build the three end-to-end experiences in [[STORIES]], then rehearse a specific behavioral question using supported details. Separate exact ownership, implemented choices, team context, uncertain metrics and proposed improvements.

## Core foundations

Normal budget: **1.5 h**; choose one connected area per session.

| Sequence | Focus | Finish condition / material |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | SQL, relational models, joins, indexes, transactions and isolation | Explain a small example; [[The Climb/Career Library/Technical Reference/SQL and Databases/SQL and Databases|SQL material]] |
| 2 | HTTP/REST, auth, pagination, retries, idempotency and background jobs | Trace one workflow and its failure paths |
| 3 | Processes/threads, concurrency, networking and caching | Explain behavior, not definitions alone; [[The Climb/Career Library/Technical Reference/Systems and Networking/Systems and Networking|Systems material]] |
| 4 | AWS execution/storage/queues, deployment and observability | Compare trade-offs using confirmed experience |
| 5 | RAG, agents/tool calling, evaluations, guardrails and prompt versioning | Explain failure cases and how to test them |
| 6 | Python/Java/Rails backend and React/frontend architecture | Pick requirements of the actual target role; don't study every framework every day |

The queue follows observed gaps and JDs, not a claim of equal expertise in every technology.

## DDIA and system design

Normal budget: **1 h**. Read one bounded section of your available edition, explain the central idea, then apply it to a sketch. DDIA's location/edition is not yet confirmed; don't invent page numbers or claim the text has been imported.

Route: reliability/scalability/maintainability → data models and storage/indexes → replication → partitioning → transactions → distributed failure/consistency → batch/stream processing.

Parallel exercises: notification service → evidence ingestion → rate limiter → tenant-isolated evidence search. Start with [[The Climb/Practice/Q10 - Notification service|notification delivery]] or [[The Climb/Practice/Q11 - Evidence ingestion|evidence ingestion]]. These are practice designs, not claimed work history.

## Daily and weekly checks

**6–8 h minimum desired prep effort**, with **6 h budgeted in the full-day routine**. Reaching 8 h needs an explicit trade-off elsewhere. Keep actual work commitments visible.

Each day leaves a solution/explanation/sketch and an exact resume point. Each week includes one cold mock and a prioritized weakness list. Record outcomes in dated evidence; don't manufacture a readiness score.
